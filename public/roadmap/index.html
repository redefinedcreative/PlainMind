Roadmap

What's now, next, and maybe.

An honest, plain view of where PlainMind is headed. No promises with dates we can't keep — just direction.

Planned, not promised. Scope moves as we learn — the honest version of a roadmap is one that admits that.

Upcoming

Next release

1.2 · in test

The organisation layer. Collections stop being something PlainMind offers you and start being something you can build and shape — and a few things that were quietly untidy get tidied.

  • Turn a search into a collection

    Collections can already make themselves from your notes. This is the other half: build a search worth keeping, pin it, and it becomes a collection that stays current on its own. Free — making a collection should not cost anything.

  • Collections introduce themselves

    Suggestions are off until you turn them on, which meant most people never met the feature. PlainMind now offers once, in your library, and only when it has actually found a phrase you wrote.

  • A collection that matches more than one phrase

    The thing you are collecting rarely has a single name. Add the others and they all count. Pro.

  • Merge two collections that overlap

    When two collections drift into holding the same saves, PlainMind notices and offers to merge them. Pro.

  • Archive a collection you're done with

    It keeps matching and keeps syncing — it is just off the list, with everything waiting when you put it back. Pro.

  • One site, one source

    Mobile and AMP versions of a page — and short links like youtu.be — group with everything else from that site instead of sitting apart. Saves you already had are tidied up too.

  • Rename a source

    Call a site whatever you actually call it, and the name follows you to your other devices. Pro.

  • Posts from social apps read properly

    The account handle and the date it went up, then the post itself. Like and comment counts are dropped — they were only ever true for the moment you saved.

  • Faster search in large libraries

    Search stays quick into the low thousands of saves and then starts to show. Groundwork now, so it never becomes something you notice.

Exploring

Real candidates with real groundwork behind them. Which of these lands — and when — depends on what people actually ask for.

  • Collection icons and ordering

    Give a collection its own symbol, and put your collections in the order you think of them.

  • PlainMind on Mac

    The same library, on a bigger screen, without a phone in the middle.

  • Search that narrows as you go

    Combine type, source and when, and see how many results each choice would leave before you commit to it.

  • Filter your library by date

    Jump to a month or a year without scrolling through everything in between.

  • Audio

    Save voice memos and audio files, play them in place, and optionally have them transcribed on your device.

  • More of what a page tells us

    Author, publication and published date, kept alongside the article text we already save.

Not planned

A roadmap is also what you decide against. These aren't backlog items we never get to — they're deliberate.

  • Tags and folders

    Filing is work you do now for a payoff you might get later. Everything sorts itself by what it is, and collections form from notes you were writing anyway.

  • Sending your library to an AI

    Collections are found by literal phrase matching, on your device. Nothing you save is sent anywhere to be analysed — that's what makes the privacy promise true rather than aspirational.

  • A feed, recommendations, or anything social

    PlainMind shows you what you saved. It will never show you what someone else saved, or decide what you should look at next.

  • A public voting board

    We tried one. It went stale, which is worse than not having it. Email is slower and more honest — and a person reads every one.

Want something that isn't here? Tell us what you're trying to do — the problem is more useful to us than the feature.
